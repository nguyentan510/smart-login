# 18.0 — Guard rails

Spec: [`../rendered-surface.md`](../rendered-surface.md). Status:
[`../refactor-plan.md`](../refactor-plan.md).

**Goal.** Land five rules before the tool exists, so the tool is built against a
statement of what it has to be able to say.

Its own suite, for the reason 16.0 and 17.0 both give: rules that are meant to
fail cannot live in a suite that blocks.

## Files

- `tests/identity/run-rendered-surface-tests.php` — new, registered `spec`
- `tests/run-all.php` — one row
- Nothing under `includes/`, `templates/` or `assets/`

## The rules

**1. The renderer exists, and it knows every account surface (18.1).** Every
`templates/partials/account/*.php` must be a named surface in
`tests/visual/render.php`. Red today at absent — the file does not exist.

The same mechanism 8.2 put in the template suite: a new partial fails the moment
it lands and passes once it has arguments. That mechanism caught `card-head` in
17.8 before anything else did.

**2. Every focusable control has an accessible name (18.2).** Render the account
surfaces, parse them as a DOM, and check every `button`, `a[href]`, `summary`,
`select`, `textarea` and visible `input` for a name: text content, `aria-label`,
`aria-labelledby`, a `<label for>`, or a `<label>` wrapped around it.

Red today at two — both the OTP code box, once per contact panel. It carries a
placeholder and nothing else.

The wrapping-`<label>` case is in the rule because leaving it out reports the
three gender radios as failures, and a rule with three false positives teaches
people to ignore it.

**3. A row-level action is at least 24 × 24 (18.3).** `.sl-action` must declare
both a `min-height` and a `min-width` of at least 24px. Red today: it declares a
height floor and no width one, and "Đổi" measures 20 × 32.

**4. Nothing points at an id that is not there, and no id is drawn twice.**
Every `aria-controls`, `aria-describedby`, `aria-labelledby` and `for` must
resolve within the rendered surface.

**Green today**, deliberately. It is the half that stops rule 2 from being
satisfied by deleting the markup that fails it, and it is the property most
likely to break silently when a partial is next split.

**5. The destructive control still works without JavaScript.** The rendered
unlink control must be a `<summary>` inside a `<details>` containing a real
`<form method="post">` with its nonce and its hidden fields.

**Green today**, and it is 17.3's unrun acceptance item turned into something
that runs. `<details>` was chosen as the mechanism precisely so this holds, and
nothing has ever checked it.

## Acceptance

- The suite lands red on rules 1, 2 and 3, and the failing output is recorded in
  the commit message
- Rules 4 and 5 pass, and the brief says why a rule that passes on the day it
  lands is worth having — the 16.0 precedent, not an oversight
- No file outside `tests/` is touched
- Every other suite comes back at its previous count — checked, not assumed

## Not in scope

Any fix, and the renderer itself. 18.1 through 18.4 are what turn these green.

### Two false positives were designed out before the rule landed

Rule 2's first draft reported five failures. Three were the gender radios, which
are named by the `<label>` wrapped around them — a shape the accessible-name
computation handles and a naive check does not. A rule with three false
positives is a rule people learn to ignore, so the wrapping-label case is in it
with the reason beside it.

What is left is the real one, twice. Everything else on the surface passes,
including what looked most likely to fail: the password eye has an `aria-label`,
every `for` finds its field, no id is drawn twice, and every `aria-controls`
resolves.

### The rules were the specification, and that was the point of writing them first

18.1 was built to satisfy rule 1 rather than rule 1 being written to describe
18.1. The surface list, the fixture source and the failure mode when a partial
is added all came out of the rule, not out of the tool.
